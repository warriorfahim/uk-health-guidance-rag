### 6. Why are chronic skin wounds in people who have recently arrived in the UK relevant to diphtheria?

- Type: in-scope
- Expected source: `diphtheria-migrant-health-guide.txt` (retrieved in top 3: yes)
- Retrieved: [1] `diphtheria-migrant-health-guide.txt`, [2] `diphtheria-migrant-health-guide.txt`, [3] `diphtheria-migrant-health-guide.txt`

**Answer:**

Chronic and long-standing wounds matter because *C. diphtheriae* can colonise or infect them, making them a site of cutaneous diphtheria rather than only respiratory disease [1]. This is particularly relevant for recent arrivals because long journeys before reaching the UK often leave people with poor skin integrity [1].

Two further points follow from this:

- Cutaneous diphtheria is a notifiable presentation on clinical suspicion, in the same way as respiratory diphtheria, so suspected cases should be reported to the local UKHSA health protection team [1].
- Wound infections in this group are often co-infections with other organisms such as Group A Streptococcus or *Staphylococcus aureus*, which complicates recognition [1].

The sources also point to dedicated guidance on this: "Skin lesions in newly arrived migrants: recognising and managing infections of public health importance" [2], and supplementary guidance on managing suspected cases, close contacts and their environment in asylum seeker accommodation settings [3].
